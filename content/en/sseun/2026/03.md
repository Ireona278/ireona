Why do I act this way? The thought occurs that I do it simply because I can; yet, at the same time, the thought arises that the reason I am able to act this way is mostly not of my own making.

What is expression? I wonder if I am calling 'expression' the very phenomenon that allows one to express through a certain form.

From my perspective, expression seems possible in any form; however, once again, the premise of being able to express in such a way seems largely independent of the person doing the expressing.

The thought also occurs to me that the meaning held by a form of expression cannot help but change every time it is expressed—that even if the same person expresses through what appears to be the same form, the meaning that form carries must change depending on _when_ it was expressed.

It seems the criterion for what makes an expression what it is must be the expresser at the moment of expression. I believe this is because it is the expresser who expresses, not the object that performs the expression.

For example, take the expression 'I don't know.' When someone produces that expression, it seems that what they want to express as 'I don't know' can only be exactly that. And if they want to express 'It’s not that I don’t know,' then that is precisely what it must be.

I think the reason I express is, first of all, because I—the one expressing—cannot help but be ignorant of most things. I admit this, too, is an expression I make because I want to express it this way. However, I believe the reason I can make such an expression is mostly not due to me. I feel that I am destined to always remain ignorant of most things regarding myself as well.

To say that I 'cannot help but be ignorant of most things' is an expression suggesting that I cannot even know the extent of what I can know or what I can be ignorant of.

What bothers me most right now is the sense that everyone is being forced to earn money.

March 25, 2026. I want to express how I feel right now through visible words.

I feel incredibly distracted. My nerves are completely on edge. I don't particularly like being in this state, but looking at my environment, it seems I have no choice but to be this way.

This is how I see it: whenever I try to move from this state toward a sense of calm, the surrounding noises catch onto my senses and set my nerves on edge again. Even when I try to escape this hyper-sensitized state, the ambient sounds pull me back into it. Then, my mood worsens. It feels as though I have to maintain this state of high tension just to make the surrounding noises slightly less unbearable?

For example, if I try to do nothing—no smartphone, no computer—and attempt something like meditation in my current home, the sound of the elevator operating in the building immediately begins to scrape at my nerves. Every time I hear it, I feel a throbbing sensation in my brain. The elevator noise is incredibly loud. It echoes throughout the entire house. It’s audible no matter which room I’m in, even with the doors closed. It’s like the sound of someone scratching a chalkboard.

And the elevator isn't everything. The moment I try to settle down, the sound of cars racing outside begins to grate on me, as does the sound of neighbors moving around. Even when I want to find peace in an environment free from repulsive sounds, I simply cannot. Such a space is not provided to me right now.

I live in what is commonly called an 'apartment.' The elevator noise seems to be the worst of it. So, I’m thinking about mentioning this to the management office. I haven't spoken to them yet, but I believe there’s a very high probability that saying something won't do any good.

There are certain responses I expect to hear, with a high degree of certainty, if I do speak up. I want to record them here. For instance, things like: 'No one else is complaining, so why are you the only one being like this?'

As I mentioned before, it does seem true that if I stay in a state of constant tension, I don’t hear things as clearly. If I keep my nerves frayed and stay frantically active, I can eventually fall asleep. But sleeping like that makes me wonder if this is the right way to live. Yesterday was exactly like that. As I write this, I haven't been awake for very long. It’s 7:57 AM. I’m noting the time as I write this part.

To describe how I feel upon waking up—I feel a bit dazed. My head aches slightly. Even while I was asleep, those sounds I mentioned must have been occurring incessantly. Just because I don't remember them doesn't mean I believe they had no effect on my body.

Here are a few more predicted responses if I bring up the elevator to the management office: 'We don't have the budget,' or 'Even if we fix it, what about the inconvenience to the residents during the construction?' Things like that.

Some might suggest, 'Why don't you just move?' It’s not that I haven't thought about it. But when I think about moving, these thoughts come to mind:

'Even if I move, can I be sure the environment there will be okay? What if it’s even worse than here?' 'Do I have enough financial leeway to move? The thought of the expenses is too burdensome.'

How utterly suffocating this is.

Then there’s the matter of 'fine dust.' According to the Korea Meteorological Administration and weather channels, the PM10 concentration is around **$89\mu g/m^3$** and PM2.5 is around **$70\mu g/m^3$**. I wonder if this is also one of the reasons why it’s hard for me to calm down and why my nerves are so on edge. It seems the fine dust levels were like this all day yesterday, too. Reaching these levels has become such a daily occurrence. Isn't this something that even moving can't solve?

Talking to the management office is simply a way of doing _something_ because staying idle is too frustrating. I don't expect them to solve anything. My mindset is just: 'Let’s at least hear what they have to say.' Even if the reaction is predictable... well, you never know.

March 29, 2026. This is Ireona. I want to express the various frustrations I am feeling today as a result of a day trip.

Regarding the date of this writing, something happened yesterday around 4:18 AM—the crack of dawn. I was fast asleep when a sudden, incredibly loud song jolted me awake. It didn't sound like someone singing live; it felt like a recorded track played at an extreme volume, much like an election campaign truck. Along with the music, I heard a rhythmic honking—something like "pa-ra-ba-ra-ba-ra-bam." It didn't last long; it was short and intense. I suspect it was a group of motorcycle gangs (bikers), the kind that roam around the neighborhood causing a disturbance. That’s what happened.

Aside from such one-off events, there is this ongoing issue. What we call "fine dust" seems to have stayed at a "Bad" level for a very long time. The high concentration has persisted for quite a while. Even now, the fine dust (PM10) is 65μg/m³ and the ultrafine dust (PM2.5) is 43μg/m³. At a rough guess, these levels have been maintained for over a week. Even on the morning I woke up to those biker sounds, the dust concentration was that high. I thought to myself, "What a perfect morning."

From my current perspective, I feel like I don’t know that much about this thing called "fine dust." People keep talking about it constantly, but I have many questions. First of all, as the name "fine dust" suggests, it isn't something that is immediately felt.

Aren't there known symptoms for when fine dust levels are severe? A stinging sensation in the parts of my body responsible for breathing? Because it persists, it feels like my insides are burning. My skepticism about people simply calling this "fine dust" is this: Is it really okay to just lump everything together and call it "fine dust"? Something like that.

Just based on this dust issue alone, I can feel that something is seriously wrong with the current situation. Why is that? Perhaps because I consider myself a living organism, an animal. As a living creature, the fact that even the basic act of breathing is this difficult feels like a grave matter. Just watching how people describe fine dust is frustrating. It’s as if they view the dust concentration as a specific stat in a video game. They seem to view the principles of why it rises and falls far too simplistically. This thought comes to mind:

In my view, the biggest reason for the increasing number of days with high fine dust is that the human population is simply too large. Yet, people today seem preoccupied with factional battles and headcount wars. They appear hell-bent on increasing their own faction's numbers just to crush those they’ve labeled as enemies. That’s how it looks to me, but the "solutions" people spout—like "let's save energy"—seem pointless. To sustain a population this size, no matter how minimally we act, fine dust is an inevitable outcome. Furthermore, the act of restraining human behavior must also be done by humans, which seems to inevitably lead to more conflict.

I recently saw the entry for "Overkill" on Namuwiki, which contained the following:

"It originated from 'excessive killing' in warfare—sacrificing too many enemies. During the Cold War, it meant possessing nuclear strike capabilities beyond what was necessary to destroy the enemy, resulting in the ability to completely crush the enemy nation or its facilities. The problem was that during the Cold War, the enemy would also strive to surpass the other's nuclear power, leading to an infinite loop and the realization of Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD)."

Reading this, I felt a connection. I thought that if you replaced "weapons" in that sentence with "human headcount," it would be just as valid. To me, the population is clearly not small, yet so many people are making a fuss, claiming it is. Far from being small, there are too many people even in my immediate surroundings. I wonder how massive the population must be that we can't accommodate everyone without building these high-rise buildings. I think the population density is nonsensical.

I feel that this fine dust situation is evidence that the human population is excessive. Do people know this or not? It’s true that this perspective isn't the mainstream one. It’s not that I don’t understand why people still claim the population is too small. I believe the reason is this: "Humans cannot distinguish between what is a mere expression and what is not." I know they say those things about headcounts because of things like "the nation" or "the economy." People immersed in those concepts think that without this many people, things won't function "normally"—or at least, that’s how I chose to think about it. To me, "economy" and "nation" are elements where the realm of "expression" is very strong. To put it differently, people talking about the economy or the nation looks no different to me than tracking specific stats in a video game.
